Hot coffee in July sounds like a punishment. But the solution isn't to switch to something bland — it's to rethink how you brew. **Summer coffee drinks** have exploded in popularity for a reason: when you chill a great single-origin coffee the right way, the flavour doesn't disappear. It transforms. Bright citrus notes come forward. Chocolate tones deepen. Sweetness opens up without a drop of sugar.

Here's why cold coffee works so well — and five recipes worth making this summer.

![Summer Coffee Drinks — cool off with easy coffee recipes, Volcano Drip](/blog/volcano-drip-summer-coffee-drinks-hero.png)

## Why Cold Coffee Has Taken Over

Walk into any café in Toronto, Vancouver, or Calgary in June and the cold brew tap is moving faster than the espresso machine. That's not a coincidence.

Heat changes how we taste. At lower temperatures, our taste buds become more sensitive to sweetness and acidity — both of which are qualities that make high-quality single-origin coffee shine. A fruity Costa Rica or a caramel-forward Guatemala that might taste subtle hot can become startlingly vibrant over ice.

Cold brewing itself changes the chemistry. When coffee is brewed cold or chilled quickly, it produces fewer of the bitter, acidic compounds that come from hot extraction. The result is smoother, sweeter, and — some studies suggest — easier on the stomach.

And then there's the obvious: on a hot day, a glass of iced coffee is simply one of the better things in life.

The key is starting with beans worth chilling. Commodity coffee and supermarket blends lose what little character they have when diluted over ice. Single-origin coffees from volcanic regions — with their distinct mineral richness and flavour complexity — hold up and often improve. For a deeper dive on method, read [cold brew vs pour over](/blog/cold-brew-vs-pour-over-single-origin-coffee).

## 5 Summer Coffee Drinks Worth Making

### Recipe 1: Iced Citrus Pour Over (The Light & Bright One)

**Best with:** [Primera Luz](/origins/costa-rica) — our Costa Rica light roast with notes of citrus, honey, and milk chocolate

This method brews hot coffee directly over ice, which chills it instantly and locks in the delicate aromatics before they can escape. It's the fastest path to a genuinely good iced coffee.

**What you need:**

- 20 g Primera Luz, medium-fine grind
- 200 ml water, just off the boil (93°C)
- 100 g ice in your serving cup

**How to make it:**

1. Place your pour over dripper over a cup or carafe already filled with 100 g of ice.
2. Add your coffee grounds and bloom with 40 ml of water for 30 seconds.
3. Pour the remaining 160 ml in slow, steady circles over 2–3 minutes.
4. The coffee hits the ice as it drips — chilling instantly.
5. Stir, add more ice if needed, and drink immediately.

**Why it works:** The rapid chill stops the extraction precisely and preserves the citrus brightness and honey sweetness that make Primera Luz what it is. No bitterness, no flatness.

**Tip:** Don't add milk — drink it black the first time. You might not go back.

### Recipe 2: Volcanic Cold Brew Concentrate

**Best with:** [Sumatra Black](/origins/indonesia) — our Indonesian dark roast with notes of earth, dark chocolate, and smoke

Cold brew and a bold dark roast are made for each other. The long, slow extraction draws out the deep chocolate and smoky notes of Sumatra Black while leaving behind any harshness. The result is rich, smooth, and endlessly versatile — drink it straight, over milk, or as a base for the next recipe.

**What you need:**

- 80 g Sumatra Black, coarse grind
- 600 ml cold filtered water
- A large jar or pitcher
- Fine mesh strainer or coffee filter

**How to make it:**

1. Combine coffee and cold water in your jar. Stir to make sure all grounds are saturated.
2. Cover and refrigerate for 16–20 hours. Longer = stronger.
3. Strain through a fine mesh strainer, then again through a coffee filter for a cleaner result.
4. Store in the fridge for up to 2 weeks.

**To serve:** Pour 1 part concentrate over ice with 1–2 parts water (or milk). Adjust to taste.

**Why it works:** Sumatra Black's earthy, chocolatey depth is exactly what you want in a cold brew. The coarse grind and slow extraction avoid over-bitterness and let the dark roast's best qualities lead.

**Tip:** The concentrate also makes an excellent base for Recipe 4 below.

### Recipe 3: Iced Caramel Volcano Latte

**Best with:** [Antigua Ember](/origins/guatemala) — our Guatemala medium roast with notes of cocoa, caramel, and subtle spice

This one is unabashedly indulgent. The natural caramel and cocoa notes in Antigua Ember do most of the heavy lifting — a small amount of maple syrup is all you need to tip it into dessert territory.

**What you need:**

- 2 shots of Antigua Ember espresso (or 80 ml strong pour over, cooled)
- 200 ml oat milk (or whole milk)
- 1 tsp pure maple syrup
- Ice
- Optional: a pinch of cinnamon

**How to make it:**

1. Brew your espresso or strong pour over and let it cool for 5 minutes (or pour over ice to chill quickly).
2. Fill a tall glass with ice. Pour in the milk.
3. Stir the maple syrup into your cooled coffee, then pour it slowly over the milk so it layers.
4. Add a pinch of cinnamon on top if you like. Stir before drinking.

**Why it works:** Antigua Ember's natural caramel notes mean you're amplifying what's already there rather than masking cheap coffee with sweetener. Maple syrup adds a Canadian depth that works surprisingly well with the subtle spice in the finish.

**Tip:** Use oat milk for a creamier texture and a slightly sweet flavour that complements the roast without overpowering it.

### Recipe 4: The Volcanic Cloud (Iced Mocha with Whipped Cream)

**Best with:** [Sumatra Black](/origins/indonesia) — our Indonesian dark roast with notes of dark chocolate, earth, and smoke

This is the indulgent one — a layered iced coffee drink with a rich syrup base, cold milk, and a cloud of whipped cream finished with shaved dark chocolate or a dusting of cinnamon. It looks impressive, takes about five minutes, and tastes like something from a specialty café.

**What you need:**

- 2 shots of Sumatra Black espresso (or 80 ml strong cold brew concentrate, cooled)
- 1–2 tbsp chocolate syrup (or caramel or vanilla — all work beautifully)
- 150 ml whole milk or oat milk
- Ice
- Whipped cream (fresh or canned)
- Shaved dark chocolate or ground cinnamon to finish

**How to make it:**

1. Drizzle your chosen syrup into the bottom of a tall glass and stir in a splash of milk to combine.
2. Fill the glass with ice, then pour in the remaining milk.
3. Slowly pour your cooled espresso or cold brew over the back of a spoon so it layers on top of the milk.
4. Pile on the whipped cream generously — this is not the place for restraint.
5. Finish with shaved dark chocolate for a mocha feel, or a pinch of cinnamon for warmth and spice. Serve immediately with a straw.

**Why it works:** Sumatra Black's natural dark chocolate character pairs perfectly with chocolate or caramel syrup — you're working with the bean, not against it. The whipped cream softens the boldness of the cold brew and makes each sip feel richer than it is. The shaved chocolate on top isn't just decoration: it adds a slightly bitter crunch that keeps the whole drink from being too sweet.

**Tip:** Chocolate syrup is the most classic pairing with Sumatra Black. But try vanilla syrup if you want something softer, or caramel if you want to echo the notes in Antigua Ember — both work brilliantly.

### Recipe 5: The Lava Shot (Espresso Tonic)

**Best with:** [Antigua Ember](/origins/guatemala) or any Volcano Drip medium roast you love as espresso

Our signature patio drink — and the one that surprises people every time. Bold espresso meets crisp tonic over ice. Bitter-sweet, effervescent, and built for summer afternoons.

**What you need:**

- 1 double shot of espresso (or 60–80 ml very strong cooled coffee)
- 4–6 oz premium tonic water
- Ice
- Orange peel for garnish

**How to make it:**

1. Fill a tall glass with ice.
2. Add tonic water.
3. Pull your espresso and pour slowly over the back of a spoon to layer on top.
4. Garnish with a fresh orange peel. Stir gently before drinking.

**Why it works:** Tonic's bitterness and bubbles lift the espresso instead of burying it — especially when the shot comes from a caramel-forward origin like Antigua Ember.

**Tip:** Add blood orange or pineapple syrup and a dehydrated orange slice for a Volcano Drip twist. Full details on our [signature drinks](/signature-drinks) page.
